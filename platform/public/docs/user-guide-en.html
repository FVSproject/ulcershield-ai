1 · Introduction

UlcerShield AI is an adaptive multi-modal intelligence platform that helps caregivers prevent pressure injuries (bedsores) before they occur. It pairs with a low-cost ESP32 sensor rig over Bluetooth Low Energy, streams pressure, temperature, humidity, and posture data in real time, and turns that stream into a personalized care plan.

The platform is designed for three environments:

  • Hospitals & ICUs — continuous bedside monitoring for immobilized patients.
  • Long-term care facilities — personalized prevention across multi-week stays.
  • Home healthcare — hospital-grade prediction in the home environment.

What makes it different

  • Digital Tissue Twin — a per-patient computational model of vulnerable tissue that keeps evolving as pressure, moisture, temperature, mobility, and clinical risk factors change.
  • Remaining Safe Tissue Time — instead of "is the pressure high?", it answers "how much longer can this tissue safely tolerate the current position?".
  • AI does not just alert, it recommends — suggests the exact repositioning direction, angle, and timing.
  • Explainable — every risk score is decomposed into its contributing factors so clinicians can trust it.
  • Personalized — takes into account age, BMI, sex, comorbidities, medications, and treatments per patient.
  • Adult and baby modes — different tissue tolerance curves, different anatomical landmarks, different 3D model.

2 · Platform overview

Roles

Every account has a role that determines what parts of the platform they see:

RoleCan seeCannot see
Patient Their own dashboard, analysis, AI insights, profile, and device pairing. Other patients' data. Admin dashboard. Patient roster.
Admin Every patient's dashboard, roster with live risk stats, add / delete patients. Nothing restricted.

Data storage

All patient records and session logs are stored locally in the browser (IndexedDB). Nothing is uploaded to a central server. The only network requests the platform makes are:

  • Load the HTML/CSS/JS from Vercel (one time, cached).
  • Call Anthropic's Claude API when you click Ask Claude, via a serverless function that keeps your API key private.

Multi-language

English, Arabic (with right-to-left layout), and Korean. Every visible string — including the AI's clinical narrative from Claude — respects the selected language.

3 · Getting started

Registering an account

1
Go to the deployed URL. Click Register here from the login page (or click Launch platform on the landing page).
2
Upload an optional profile picture. Enter username, password, full name.
3
Choose the patient type — Adult or Baby. This selection changes the entire risk algorithm and the 3D body model. Adult is default (13 years and older); Baby is for anyone under 2 years.
4
Fill in age, sex, height, weight. All optional, but they personalize the risk model — omitting them means the algorithm applies neutral modifiers.
5
Tick any existing conditions: diabetes, peripheral vascular disease, cardiac disease, renal failure, malnutrition, neuropathy or spinal cord injury, incontinence, dementia, active cancer. Add anything else in the free-text field.
6
Tick current medications: corticosteroids, vasoconstrictors, anticoagulants, chronic sedatives, chronic NSAIDs. Add other drugs in the free-text field.
7
Tick special treatments: mechanical ventilation, dialysis, chemotherapy, radiation therapy, cast or traction, feeding tube.
8
Add clinical notes (mobility, existing wounds, care team notes).
9
Click Create account.
First-user tip: register as admin. The very first account in a browser sees a "Register as admin" toggle at the top of the form. Check it to create the administrator account. Every subsequent account is a normal patient.

Signing in

Return visits: enter your username and password on /login. Admins are routed to the admin dashboard; patients go directly to their personal dashboard.

Demo mode

The login page has a "Continue as demo patient" button. It creates a pre-filled simulated patient (74 y F, 162 cm, 58 kg) so you can explore the platform without registering.

5 · Personalized dashboard

The main clinical view. Every card is live — updated at 1 Hz from the sensor stream (real BLE or simulator).

Header

Shows the page title ("Personalized Patient Care Dashboard"), a subtitle, and a connection badge in the top-right. The badge colors change based on the sensor source:

  • BLE linked — paired with an ESP32 device via Bluetooth.
  • Simulator — synthetic data source running (default when no hardware).
  • Connecting… — pairing in progress.
  • Disconnected — no data stream.

Click the badge to open the /connect page for pairing.

Patient card

Avatar, full name, ID, age, sex, height, weight, and the live status band (LOW / MODERATE / HIGH / CRITICAL) with the numeric risk score (0–100).

AI Recommendation

The single most important card. Shows the current recommended action (e.g. "Reposition patient 30° to the left now") with:

  • Tone badge: advisory · act soon · urgent.
  • Why — bullet list of the specific signals that drove the recommendation (e.g. "Peak 78 mmHg on sacrum", "Humidity 68%").
  • Apply recommendation button — records that the caregiver acted on it.
  • Estimated remaining safe time — how long the current position stays safe.

Pressure zones (Left / Right)

Live readouts from the two FSR sensors, showing force in Newtons, pressure in mmHg (primary), and grams-equivalent. Each zone displays:

  • Level badge: SAFE (<32 mmHg), ELEVATED (32–60), HIGH (60–100), CRITICAL (>100), or SATURATED (sensor bottomed out).
  • Bar with clinical tick marks at 32 (capillary closing), 60 (elevated), 100 (critical).
  • Peak this session — the highest mmHg reading since the counter was last reset.

Body pressure map (2D)

An SVG silhouette of the supine patient with 8 pressure spots (shoulders, scapulae, hips, heels — left and right). Each spot pulses; size and color follow the estimated pressure at that anatomical location.

Vitals & microclimate

Body temperature (MLX90614 IR) and humidity (DHT22). Each row shows the value, unit, sensor name, and a clinical status message — e.g. "Elevated — inspect for redness" at > 37.5 °C.

Repositioning ring

Circular progress ring showing minutes since the last detected repositioning. The ring fills toward the 2-hour target and turns amber > 75 % and red at 100 %. Adjacent stats: turn count, target minutes, occupancy, and a center-of-pressure indicator showing whether the patient is leaning left, balanced, or right.

Recommendations list

Rule-based guidance derived from the local risk model (independent of Claude). Always visible; updates in real time. Contains up to 15 short cards covering:

  • Primary posture guidance (turn to right, turn to left, return to supine).
  • Reposition timer (soon, now).
  • Pressure-driven advice (peak > 100 mmHg, saturation).
  • Asymmetry warning.
  • Humidity / temperature alerts.
  • Standing reminders: offload heels, keep head of bed ≤ 30°.

Event timeline

Ordered list of the last 40 sensor events — turns detected, calibration commands, resets, sensor connect/disconnect. Each row shows a colored kind indicator, human-readable text, and timestamp with uptime.

Calibration & controls

Four buttons that send commands to the ESP32 over BLE:

ButtonEffect
Zero (unload)Records the no-load baseline for both pads. Do this with nothing on the sensors.
Calibrate LeftRecords the force-to-resistance constant for FSR A. Place a known 1 kg mass on the left pad first.
Calibrate RightSame for FSR B.
Reset countersZeroes the turn count, dose, and peak pressure values.

When running in simulator mode, the buttons show an "Simulator active — connect via BLE" toast instead of executing.

6 · 3D Digital Tissue Twin

An interactive 3D anatomical model of the patient lying supine on a hospital bed. Six pressure hotspots overlay the vulnerable anatomical regions and pulse in proportion to their live risk score.

Adult mode

  • Full-size anatomical mannequin (~1.7 m).
  • Six hotspots: shoulders, sacrum, left hip, right hip, left heel, right heel.
  • Sacrum is weighted most heavily (dominant load region in adult supine).

Baby mode

  • Newborn proportions (~50 cm long, head:body ratio ≈ 1:4 vs 1:8 in adults).
  • Cot-sized bed, diaper covering.
  • Seven hotspots including occiput (back of head) — the #1 pressure landmark in infants due to relatively larger head and soft skull.

Controls

  • Click and drag — rotate the camera around the body.
  • Scroll — zoom in / out.
  • Show labels / Hide labels — toggle the region name badges. Labels sit outside the body silhouette so they never cover it.
  • Reset view (circular arrow icon) — return to the default camera angle.

Hotspot behavior

Each hotspot has a solid dot and a soft glow. Both pulse at a frequency and intensity proportional to the region's risk score:

  • Low risk → slow, faint pulse.
  • Moderate → medium pulse.
  • High → fast, bright pulse.
  • Critical → very fast, intense pulse (attention-grabbing).

Colors follow the risk band: green (safe), amber (elevated), orange (high), red (critical).

Custom 3D model (optional)

To use a photoreal medical model instead of the procedural anatomical mannequin, drop a glTF/GLB file at public/models/body.glb. The platform probes for this file at runtime; if present it loads instead of the procedural body. Recommended sources: MakeHuman, Blender Studio Base Meshes (CC0), NIH 3D Print Exchange, Sketchfab CC0.

7 · Remaining Safe Tissue Time

The most clinically meaningful number on the dashboard. Instead of asking "is the pressure high?", it asks "how much longer can this tissue safely tolerate the current position?".

The card

A circular countdown showing minutes remaining until the highest-risk region crosses into the high-risk band. The color follows the number:

  • SAFE — > 45 min remaining.
  • SOON — 16 to 45 min remaining.
  • IMMEDIATE — 1 to 15 min remaining.
  • ACT NOW — 0 min remaining.

The "Why this number?" panel

Expandable section below the countdown. Shows:

  • Base minutes at the current peak pressure (from the Reswick-Rogers curve).
  • Every applied modifier with its multiplicative factor:
    • Age band (× 0.55 to × 1.00).
    • BMI band (× 0.70 to × 1.00).
    • Sex (× 0.98 for post-menopausal, else neutral).
    • Each existing condition (× 0.60 to × 0.90).
    • Each medication (× 0.75 to × 0.95).
    • Each treatment (× 0.70 to × 0.90).
    • Live microclimate (skin temp, humidity).
  • Total factor and final minutes.
Worked example. A 74-year-old woman, BMI 22, with diabetes and incontinence, on corticosteroids, whose sacrum has just reached 85 mmHg with skin 37.7 °C and humidity 68 %:
Base 4200 / (85 − 32) = 79 min · age × 0.75 · diabetes × 0.75 · incontinence × 0.80 · steroids × 0.85 · skin > 37.5 °C × 0.90 · humidity 68 % × 0.85
Combined = 0.29 → 23 min remaining.

See §14 for the full algorithm derivation and citations.

8 · Risk index (5 axes)

A 100-point score computed on-device (not from Claude) that provides a fast summary of overall risk. It decomposes into five explainable axes:

AxisMaxDriven by
Pressure30Peak mmHg among the two zones.
Immobility30Minutes since last detected reposition, versus the 120-min target.
Moisture15Relative humidity above 40 %.
Temperature15Skin temperature above 37.5 °C (or below 33 °C).
Asymmetry10Center-of-pressure imbalance above 0.4 (fully occupied bed).

The risk card shows the total as a circular progress ring and each axis as a horizontal bar so a caregiver can immediately see why the score is what it is. Band thresholds:

  • LOW · 0–24
  • MODERATE · 25–49
  • HIGH · 50–74
  • CRITICAL · 75–100

Risk trajectory

A separate card that projects the highest-risk region forward across five horizons: Now, 15 min, 1 h, 3 h, 6 h. Assumes the current posture holds. A dashed vertical marker shows when repositioning is recommended.

Risk by body area

Per-region breakdown (sacrum, left hip, right hip, left heel, right heel, shoulders, plus occiput for babies). Each row shows a colored band + numeric score. An expandable "Why the risk is what it is" panel lists the underlying contributors.

9 · AI insights (Claude)

The platform calls Anthropic's Claude model on demand to produce a clinician-grade narrative interpretation of the current sensor snapshot.

How to trigger

1
Open the dashboard or /ai page.
2
Wait for at least one sensor reading (a second or two).
3
Click Ask Claude on the AI card.
4
The button changes to "Analyzing…" for 3–8 seconds while the API responds.
5
The response renders in place. Click Re-analyze to run it again on the latest snapshot.

What comes back

A structured JSON response, parsed and rendered as:

  • Summary — one paragraph explaining the current clinical picture.
  • Reasoning bullets — up to 6 short items citing specific measured values.
  • Actions — up to 6 recommended next steps, each with a tone (info / warn / danger).
  • Escalation flag — surfaces as a red badge when the model believes the patient needs a clinician now.

Language

The narrative respects the currently selected UI language. Switch to Arabic or Korean via the header language button, click Re-analyze, and every user-visible field returns in that language. Numeric values, sensor names (FSR, MLX90614, DHT22) and units (mmHg, °C, %RH) stay in Latin script for clarity.

Model choice

Defaults to claude-sonnet-4-6. Change by setting the ANTHROPIC_MODEL environment variable to any current Claude model (e.g. claude-opus-4-7 for higher intelligence at higher cost).

Cost

Each request is roughly ~1500 input tokens (largely cached across calls) plus ~600 output tokens. On Sonnet 4.6 pricing, that is approximately USD 0.01 per analysis. 100 analyses per day is around USD 30 per month.

Privacy note. The current sensor snapshot and the patient profile object are sent to Anthropic each time you click "Ask Claude". Do not click if the patient profile contains data you cannot share with a third-party API.

10 · Admin dashboard

Only visible to accounts with the admin role. Accessed at /admin.

KPI strip

Four tiles at the top:

  • Total patients — count of non-admin accounts.
  • Active (5 min) — patients whose last log row is within the last 5 minutes.
  • High risk — patients whose current risk score ≥ 50.
  • Avg risk — mean risk across all patients.

Auto-refreshes every 10 seconds so the numbers stay current.

Patient roster

Full list of patients, one row each. Every row shows:

  • Avatar + name + @username + age.
  • Sample count (how many log rows exist for this patient).
  • Current risk score with color-coded band.
  • Last-seen time and an online indicator dot (green = active within 5 min).
  • View button — opens that patient's dashboard.
  • Delete button — removes the patient and their log rows (requires confirmation).

A search box filters by name, username, or email.

Impersonation (viewing another patient)

When an admin clicks View on a patient row, the dashboard opens with ?patient=<id> in the URL. A cyan "Viewing patient · [name]" banner appears at the top of every dashboard card, with a "Return to admin" button.

All data on the page — pressure, risk, AI narrative, analysis logs — is scoped to that patient. Log rows are persisted to that patient's ID, not the admin's.

Patients cannot use ?patient= to view someone else's data — the URL parameter is stripped and they are always scoped to their own session.

12 · Device connection (BLE)

Accessed at /connect. The bridge between the platform and the ESP32 sensor rig.

Bluetooth Low Energy pairing

Available in Chrome, Edge, and Opera on desktop and Android. Not available on iOS Safari — Apple has never shipped Web Bluetooth. A warning banner appears if the browser lacks support.

1
Flash the firmware at firmware/BedsorePredictorBLE.ino to your ESP32 (see §17).
2
Power on the ESP32 within Bluetooth range (~10 m).
3
Click Pair device. The browser opens a native pairing dialog.
4
Select UlcerShield-01 from the list. The connection indicator turns green.
5
Live pressure, vitals, and turn events start streaming to every open tab.

Simulator

Right-hand card. Streams synthetic sensor data at 1 Hz — periodic posture shifts, slowly warming skin, drifting humidity. Use it to demo the platform without hardware or when the device is out of range. Click Start simulator or Restart simulator.

14 · The algorithm in detail

The Remaining Safe Tissue Time and personalized risk calculations are derived from published clinical work. Full derivation with citations is in docs/algorithm.md in the source repository.

Base curve

An inverse pressure–time damage curve, calibrated against Landis (1930), Kosiak (1959, 1961), and Reswick & Rogers (1976):

baseSafeMin(P) = K / max(1, P − P_cap)      (bounded at ceiling)
ParameterAdultBaby (< 2 y)
P_cap · capillary closing pressure32 mmHg20 mmHg
K · tissue-tolerance constant42002500
Ceiling · max safe minutes240180

Every modifier is multiplicative. The product is clamped to [0.20, 1.20]. Weights derived from the Braden Scale (Bergstrom 1987) and the Waterlow Score (1985):

15 · Privacy & data

  • All patient records live in the browser. IndexedDB (via Dexie). Nothing is uploaded to a central server.
  • Log rows are per-patient. Analysis charts only show the active viewing patient's data.
  • Patient sessions cannot see other patients. The ?patient= URL parameter is stripped for non-admin sessions.
  • Admin sessions can see everything. Impersonation via ?patient=<id> writes log rows against the impersonated patient, not the admin.
  • The Claude API call sends the current snapshot and the patient profile object to Anthropic. No historical logs are sent. See the Claude privacy note in §9.
  • Session ends when you sign out. Patient records persist; log rows persist; the in-memory session token is cleared.
  • Cross-device. Because records are per-browser, they do not sync between devices. To move data, use the CSV export on the analysis page.

17 · ESP32 firmware (BLE)

Reference implementation at firmware/BedsorePredictorBLE.ino.

Hardware

  • ESP32 dev board (any variant with BLE).
  • Two Force-Sensitive Resistors (FSR402 or similar) with 10 kΩ voltage divider each. Wired to GPIO 34 (left) and GPIO 35 (right).
  • DHT22 humidity sensor on GPIO 4 with a 10 kΩ pull-up.
  • MLX90614 IR temperature sensor on I²C (SDA = GPIO 21, SCL = GPIO 22).

Required libraries (Arduino IDE)

  • NimBLE-Arduino (h2zero).
  • ArduinoJson (Benoit Blanchon).
  • DHT sensor library (Adafruit).
  • Adafruit MLX90614 Library.

Calibration procedure

1
Power on the ESP32 with no load on either pad.
2
Pair the device with the platform.
3
Click Zero (unload) on the dashboard. This records the no-load resistance baseline.
4
Place a known 1 kg mass on a rigid disc over the LEFT pad.
5
Click Calibrate Left. Records the force-to-resistance constant for FSR A.
6
Repeat for the RIGHT pad with Calibrate Right.
7
The device is now calibrated. Constants are held in RAM until next power cycle.

Serial commands (115200 baud)

Available in addition to BLE commands:

  • c — zero calibration.
  • 1 — mass calibration, zone A.
  • 2 — mass calibration, zone B.
  • r — reset turn counter and doses.

19 · Troubleshooting

The AI panel returns "AI request failed"

Check:

  • HTTP 501 — ANTHROPIC_API_KEY is missing on the server. Add it in Vercel → Settings → Environment Variables, then redeploy.
  • HTTP 401 — key is invalid or was rotated. Regenerate at console.anthropic.com and update the env var.
  • HTTP 429 — rate limited. Wait a moment and try again.

The Pair device button is disabled

Your browser does not support Web Bluetooth. Use Chrome, Edge, or Opera on desktop or Android. iOS Safari does not expose Web Bluetooth.

The dashboard shows "No live data"

The simulator should auto-start on the first visit. If nothing appears, go to /connect and click Start simulator.

Labels cover the 3D body

Labels are hidden by default. If you enabled them and they overlap, click Hide labels — the pulsing hotspots alone convey the risk band.

Analysis page shows "No data yet"

Log rows are persisted every 5 seconds while the dashboard is open. Wait a minute after opening the dashboard, then return to analysis.

The AI narrative is in the wrong language

Click Re-analyze after switching the language. Existing responses are cached client-side and do not re-translate automatically.
